Cache-Aligned Resolution: Coarse-Attention on Cache-Miss Steps

A measured, paired evaluation of restricting expensive video-attention compute to a coarse spatial grid — plus a saved high-frequency anchor — on the genuine full-compute steps that MiniMax-H3's caching schedule cannot skip. Real end-to-end speedup, below the predeclared ship gate.

Executive summary

What changed, what it cost, what it bought

MiniMax-H3's order-1 cascading cache already skips most attention/MLP/residual compute across the 50-step denoise schedule. This experiment narrows scope further: on the steps the cache cannot skip — genuine full-compute steps inside blocks [10, 40) — the video-attention context is computed on a coarse 10×18 spatial grid instead of the dense 24×42 grid, with a σ-scaled saved high-frequency anchor added back to limit detail loss. Cache-hit timesteps and blocks outside that range are left completely untouched.

Across 20 paired prompt/seed runs, end-to-end latency dropped from 33.028 ± 2.236 s to 32.235 ± 1.959 s — a 1.0245× speedup (2.4% lower E2E time), faster on 15 of 20 pairs, paired t(19) = 3.70, p = .0015. Denoise-only time moved from 28.821 s to 28.088 s (1.0258×). The effect is statistically real and directionally consistent, but it misses the predeclared 3% ship gate, and visual fidelity is content-dependent: static/low-motion scenes are pixel-identical, while fine-detail, high-motion scenes (e.g. wuxia choreography) show visible softening. This method is not proposed for PR in its current form.

Method

Where the change applies, and what it does there

The cache's order-1 policy (τ = .80, 6-step warmup, 12-step horizon, cap 10) already decides, step by step, whether to reuse its cached residual payload and skip the DiT stack or run a genuine compute. This method does not touch that decision. It only changes genuine DiT computations after an eight-step coarse-attention warmup, and only inside transformer blocks [10, 40).

exact
Blocks 10–39 — coarse video attention on genuine computes
Blocks 40–49
exact
full attention hidden / Q / MLP / residual / output remain full resolution full attention

Inside the modified block range

At each timestep, the cascade still decides whether the entire DiT is a cache hit or a genuine full compute. Only genuine computes reach the modified blocks. The pattern below is illustrative of that mix — not a literal per-step trace — and shows what changes on each kind of step.

Genuine full-compute step — coarse attention + anchor applied Cache-hit step — untouched, reused

What changes on a genuine full-compute step

Hidden states, Q projection, MLP, residual add, and output projection are all still computed in full — nothing there is approximated. The only change is the spatial resolution of the video-attention context used inside that full computation:

Reference: dense context
24 × 42
→
Candidate: coarse context
10 × 18
σ-scaled high-frequency anchor: a prior exact attention computation saves the high-frequency component of the full attention output. Later coarse computations upsample their output and restore that saved component, scaled by the current denoising σ. This is what keeps low-motion, low-detail content close to pixel-identical (see Fidelity) while the coarse grid does the work of cutting attention cost on the steps that matter for speed.

Content-dependent fidelity

The same method, two very different outcomes

Aggregate fidelity numbers hide a real split: content that is spatially low-frequency and low-motion is essentially unaffected by coarsening the attention grid, while content with fine high-frequency detail and fast motion loses visibly more than the anchor term can restore.

Metric glossary

What each number in this report actually measures

SSIM (structural similarity)
Compares local luminance, contrast, and structure between candidate and reference frames on a 0–1 scale. 1.0 is pixel-for-pixel identical structure; values below ~0.90 are visible to the eye as softening, texture loss, or drift.
LPIPS (learned perceptual image patch similarity)
A learned distance between deep-network features of candidate and reference frames, closer to how a human perceives difference than pixel metrics. Lower is more similar; 0 is identical.
PSNR (peak signal-to-noise ratio)
A pixel-error metric in decibels — higher is closer to the reference. It is dominated by exact matches (100 dB on the pixel-identical ocean prompt), which is why the aggregate mean and std look unusually wide; treat it as a secondary check to SSIM/LPIPS rather than the primary fidelity signal.
Sharpness ratio
Candidate high-frequency detail energy divided by reference. 1.0 means equally sharp; below 1 means the candidate is softer — expected from grid coarsening before the anchor correction.
Temporal jitter ratio
Compares frame-to-frame change magnitude between candidate and reference. Values near 1.0 mean motion stability is preserved; deviation indicates the candidate is flickering or smoothing motion differently than the reference.
Patch boundary ratio
Measures edge energy specifically at coarse-grid patch boundaries relative to reference — a direct check for visible seams introduced by the 10×18 downsampling.
Waveform / spectral correlation, LSD (audio)
Waveform and spectral correlation measure how closely the candidate audio track tracks the reference in time and frequency (1.0 = identical). Log-spectral distance (LSD, dB) is a frequency-domain error measure — lower is closer. All three confirm this change barely touches audio.
Paired t-test (statistic, p-value)
Each of the 20 prompt/seed pairs contributes one reference-minus-candidate delta; the paired t-test asks whether the mean of those deltas is reliably different from zero, accounting for per-pair variance. p < .05 means the timing difference is unlikely to be noise — it says nothing about whether the size of the effect clears a ship bar.

Limitations

  • Below the predeclared ship gate. The 3% end-to-end speedup threshold was set before this run. The measured 2.4% mean is statistically real (p = .0015) but does not clear that bar, and per-prompt speedup ranges from −0.17% to +4.03% — the gate would need either a larger average effect or a narrower content scope to justify shipping as-is.
  • Fidelity is content-dependent, not uniform. Low-motion, low-frequency scenes (ocean) are unaffected; fast, fine-detail scenes (wuxia) drop to SSIM 0.7924 / LPIPS ~0.28 — well below the aggregate mean and likely visible to viewers.
  • Small, fixed matrix. 5 prompts × 4 seeds on one GPU is enough to establish a real paired effect but not enough to characterize the full space of content types this method would touch in production.
  • One additional comparison was excluded from every result in this report. A further prompt/seed attempt failed during launch before either run completed cleanly, corrupting its timing capture; the delta it would have produced was not representative of the method and is omitted here rather than reported.
  • PSNR is a poor aggregate signal for this workload. It is dominated by pixel-identical runs pulling the mean and std to extreme values; SSIM and LPIPS are the more trustworthy fidelity read.
  • Denoise-only and end-to-end tell slightly different stories. The gap between the two speedups reflects fixed encode/decode overhead outside the modified window; the ship gate is defined on end-to-end time, which is the harder number to move.
  • Cache and coarse-grid hyperparameters were not swept. τ, warmup, horizon, cap, the 10×18 target resolution, and the σ-scaling schedule were all held at one configuration; it is unknown whether a different point in that space clears the gate without the wuxia-class fidelity cost.

Decision

✕ Not proposed for PR

Real, statistically significant, and mechanically sound — but it misses the ship gate agreed before this run, and the content-dependent fidelity cost is concentrated exactly where it would be most visible.

Recommendation: keep this as a validated research direction, not a shipping candidate. The next useful step is closing the gap on high-motion, fine-detail content — either a less aggressive coarsening ratio or a stronger anchor term for those cases — before re-running against the same gate, rather than lowering the gate to fit this result.
